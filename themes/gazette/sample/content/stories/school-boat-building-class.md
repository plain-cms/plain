WESTHAVEN — Four years after the council came within one vote of cutting it
as “vocationally obsolete”, Harbour Academy’s boat-building course is
oversubscribed, with 62 applicants for 24 benches this September.

The two-year course, taught in a shed the school leases from the harbour
board for a pound a year, has students build a traditional clinker dinghy
from oak and larch, and then sail what they built. Every leaver for the
past three years has gone into work or further training, most of them
within sight of the school: the sail loft, the chandlery, the harbour
board’s own workshop.

“The argument in 2022 was that nobody builds wooden boats any more,” said
teacher and former shipwright Callum Voss. “The argument now is a waiting
list. I prefer the new argument.”

The course’s revival tracks the quay’s. The estuary co-operative landed its
first oysters this month, and the east quay yard is bidding for its largest
contract in a generation. A generation is discovering that the harbour is
not a museum.

The school will add an evening class for adults in January. There is
already a waiting list for that, too.
