WESTHAVEN — The storm drain cover on Foundry Lane, grey and ignored since
the day it was installed, was found on Tuesday morning transformed into a
mosaic of a diving cormorant: the seventh work by the anonymous tiler the
town has taken to calling the Grouter.

The pattern is consistent. A piece of overlooked municipal ironwork or
concrete is tiled overnight in weatherproof mosaic; the subject is always a
local sea bird, and the work is always technically excellent. Earlier
pieces include the bollards outside the library (herring gulls, 2024) and
the bus shelter on Marsh Road (oystercatchers, 2025), now a minor
destination.

The council’s stance has evolved. The first mosaic was logged for removal
as vandalism; it survived when the removal quote exceeded the cost of
leaving it. By the fourth, the highways team was quietly sealing the works.
This week a council spokesperson said only: “We would welcome a
conversation about grout specifications for the marine environment.”

The Grouter has never claimed a work or made contact. The only signature
is a single blue tile, always bottom left.

The bookmakers’ favourite theories — the art teacher, the retired dentist,
the entire Thursday film club — remain unconfirmed. By Friday the cormorant
had its own postcard in the harbour gift shop.
