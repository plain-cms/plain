WESTHAVEN — The lifeboat station recorded two callouts in May, its quietest
May since 1986 and a number the crew attributes, unglamorously, to flags.

The colour-coded bathing flags, introduced at the town beach and Pelham
sands last summer after a bad August, mark the day’s currents at three
points swimmers actually pass — the car park, the café and the waterline —
rather than on one board by the slipway that nobody read.

“Rescues don’t usually start in the water,” said volunteer coxswain Leah
Brand, a schoolteacher who has crewed the boat for eleven years. “They
start in the car park, when someone’s already decided it’s a swimming day.
That’s where the information has to be.”

Both callouts were minor: a drifting inflatable (empty; its owner was
ashore eating chips) and a kayaker with a dislocated shoulder, brought in
within the hour.

The station’s fundraising target this year is a new launch tractor. The
quiet month, Brand notes, does not lower the target: “The tractor doesn’t
care how good May was.”
