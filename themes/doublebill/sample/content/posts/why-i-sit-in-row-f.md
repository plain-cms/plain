Readers ask about the seat more than about any film. So, once and for all.

The Fossgate has fourteen rows, A to N, and a screen a little under eight metres wide. The old rule of thumb says you should sit about one and a half screen-widths back, so that the picture fills your eye without asking you to turn your head. At the Fossgate that puts you in row F.

Seat 9 is because the projector beam is not quite central. The booth was built in 1937 for a different screen, and the picture lands a few centimetres left of the room’s middle line. F9 is in the middle of the picture, not the room.

## The objections

- *Row F is too close for subtitles.* It is not. You read them the way you read a road sign: without moving.
- *The aisle seats are easier.* They are, and you watch the film at an angle.
- *You should sit at the back, like a critic.* Critics sit at the back so they can leave.

At the Bootham Electric, by the way, it is row H in screens 1 and 2 and row E in the small one. I am not as fond of any of them.
