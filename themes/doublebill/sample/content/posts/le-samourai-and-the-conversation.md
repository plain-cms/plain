Whoever programmes the Fossgate’s double bills has a gift, and this one is the proof.

| Film | Year | Runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Le Samouraï, by Jean-Pierre Melville | 1967 | 105 min |
| The Conversation, by Francis Ford Coppola | 1974 | 113 min |

In the first, Alain Delon plays a hired killer who lives in a grey room with a caged bird and adjusts his hat brim in the mirror before every job. In the second, Gene Hackman plays a surveillance expert who records other people’s secrets for a living and keeps his own telephone number from his girlfriend.

Seen together, they are the same film made seven years apart on two continents: a man so good at his work that the work has replaced him. Melville films it as ritual, almost silently. Coppola films it as paranoia, with a soundtrack that keeps replaying the same three seconds of tape until you hear them in your sleep.

Both are restorations, both look immaculate, and the interval is just long enough to buy a coffee and wonder whose side you were on.

Saturday 3 October from 6pm. Le Samouraï is in French with English subtitles.
