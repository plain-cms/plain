Everyone thinks they know *Brief Encounter*. Clipped voices, a railway platform, two married people who meet every Thursday and do not, in the end, do anything about it. It has been parodied so often that I sat down at the Fossgate half ready to laugh.

I did not laugh. Seen on a big screen, from the new restoration, it is a film about how much feeling can fit into a cup of tea in a station buffet, and the answer is all of it.

Celia Johnson’s face does most of the work. Lean keeps cutting back to her in close-up while her own voice tells us what she is thinking, and the gap between the calm face and the frantic voice is the whole film.

It is 86 minutes long, in black and white, and the Fossgate plays it again on Sunday 6 September at 2.30pm. Take a handkerchief, and do not let anyone tell you it is quaint.
