An elderly couple take the train from Onomichi to Tokyo to see their grown-up children. The children are pleased to see them, and busy, and within two days have arranged to send them to a spa resort to get them out of the way.

That is nearly all of *Tokyo Story*, and it is devastating precisely because nobody in it is cruel. Ozu keeps his camera low, at the height of someone kneeling on a tatami mat, and lets every scene run until you notice who is not listening.

> “Isn’t life disappointing?” “Yes, it is.”

The exchange comes near the end, between the youngest daughter and the widowed daughter-in-law, and both women smile as they say it. I have seen the film five times and that smile still undoes me.

The Fossgate is showing a 35mm print on loan from a film archive, slightly soft in the first reel and lovely after that. Sunday 25 October at 2.30pm, 136 minutes, Japanese with English subtitles. Take someone who is a long train ride away from their parents.
