The café is two hundred metres underground, in a chamber carved out of rock salt, and in winter it has perhaps a dozen visitors a day. Agnieszka Wrona’s second feature spends a season there with the Nowak family, who run it: a mother, two adult sons, and a grandfather who sweeps a floor that never gets dirty.

There is no crisis in *The Salt Year*. One son wants to leave for Gdańsk. The lift breaks for a week. A school party arrives and the family become, for an afternoon, terrifyingly efficient. That is all, and Wrona makes every bit of it matter.

The film is shot in long, still frames lit by the café’s own lamps, and the salt walls glow a colour I have no word for. Halfway through I realised I had stopped waiting for something to happen and started simply living there.

It runs 121 minutes, in Polish with English subtitles, and it is the film from this year I expect to remember longest.

Showing at the Bootham Electric until Thursday 15 October.
