For an hour *Harbour Lights* is very good. A customs officer, Marta, finds a container that should not be on the dock at Alcântara, and Tomás Ferreira builds the first act with real skill: wet cobbles, sodium light, a phone call that ends too soon.

Then the film decides to be about something else. A brother appears. There is a second container, then a third. By the final half-hour I had stopped following who was paying whom, and so, I suspect, had the script.

What keeps it worth seeing is Inês Cardoso as Marta. She plays the whole film as a woman doing sums in her head, and in the long scene where she reads a ship’s manifest aloud, she makes paperwork into suspense.

It runs 128 minutes, which is twenty too many. In Portuguese with English subtitles, at the Bootham Electric until Thursday 24 September.
