The first loaf comes out of the oven forty minutes in. Until then Ilse Maaskant has shown us the dough, the clock, the delivery van idling in the rain, and three people who have worked nights together for so long they no longer need to finish a sentence.

*Small Hours* runs 104 minutes and never leaves the bakery. Nothing much happens to the bakers in it. Their owner wants to sell, one of them has a son who will not answer his phone, and the radio plays the shipping forecast in Dutch. That is the plot, and it is enough.

What Maaskant understands is that night work has its own tempo, and she films at it. Shots last as long as a task takes. You start to know, a beat before anyone moves, that the trays are about to come out.

## Who it is for

Not everyone. The woman behind me left after twenty minutes, and I understood why. But if you have ever worked a shift that ended at dawn, this is the only film I know that has been there.

Showing at the Bootham Electric until Thursday 12 November. Dutch, with English subtitles. Go to the 9pm.
