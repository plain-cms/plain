Morven Baird spent a year in the passenger seat of a red van. The round it drives is nine kilometres long, has forty-one stops and, the film tells us at the start, will be cut at the end of the year.

You expect a lament, and *Nine Kilometres to Tarbet* is not one. It is a portrait of a road, built from the two minutes a day the postwoman, Senga, spends at each door. A retired ferryman who waits at his gate with tea. A family whose parcels are always the same size. A woman who writes to herself, because then someone comes.

Baird never interviews anyone. The talk is whatever happens at the door, and the film is edited so tightly around it that the seasons change in a cut.

It is 88 minutes long and does not waste one of them. The last stop, in December, is the best scene I have watched this year: a whole life in a handshake through a van window.

Showing at the Bootham Electric until Thursday 29 October, with a question-and-answer session after the Saturday matinee.
