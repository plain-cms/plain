The Fossgate’s late-summer double bill is a long afternoon, and a generous one.

*Alice in the Cities* (1974, 110 minutes) is black and white, and German with English subtitles. A journalist who cannot write his piece about America is left in charge of a nine-year-old girl, and the two of them drive around the Ruhr looking for her grandmother’s house from a photograph.

*Paris, Texas* (1984, 145 minutes) is in colour so saturated it feels like weather. A man walks out of the desert after four years, silent, and his brother drives him back to the son he left behind.

Seen together they rhyme constantly: men who are better with children than with adults, roads instead of homes, and endings that let somebody go. The first is lighter and funnier than its reputation. The second still has the best scene Wenders ever shot, a conversation through a one-way mirror that runs for nine minutes and holds the whole room.

Saturday 29 August from 2pm, with an interval long enough for tea.
