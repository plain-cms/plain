On Sunday 13 September the Fossgate showed *A Matter of Life and Death* from a 35mm print, and for the first ten minutes I wondered whether it was a mistake.

The print is old. There is a scratch down the right of the frame through most of reel four, and the black-and-white heaven looks a little grey. Then David Niven wakes on an English beach, the film turns to Technicolor, and two hundred people stopped rustling at once.

## Why a print matters

A digital copy of this film is sharper. It is also flatter, because the restoration evens out what the 1946 lab could not. On film, the rhododendrons at the camera obscura are almost too red to look at, and the great escalator to the other world has the slight shimmer of something actually photographed.

The story — a pilot who should have died, arguing his case before a celestial court — is as daft and as moving as ever. Roger Livesey steals it, as he steals everything.

The film society plans to show the print again in the spring. If you have only seen it on a television, go then.
