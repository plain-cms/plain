Kiln Row is three potters sharing two kilns in a railway arch. Their work was selling — mostly to people who had already held it at a market. Online, where every pot was a phone picture on a windowsill, it was not selling at all.

## The challenge

Glazes are the whole point of the work and the hardest thing about it to show: the same celadon reads green, grey or blue depending on the light. The potters needed a way to present each firing honestly, twice a year, without hiring us twice a year to do it.

## The approach

We built them a method rather than a shoot. One table, one north window, one grey card, and a printed sheet of camera settings taped to the wall of the arch. Each pot is photographed three ways — whole, in the hand, and the foot — and laid out in a catalogue template that is all white space and a single line of glaze notes written by whoever made it. We shot the first edition alongside them; they shot the second on their own.

## The outcome

Online orders overtook market sales within the first season. Returns for "not the colour I expected" fell from roughly one order in eight to one in fifty, and the spring catalogue was produced entirely in-house in two days.
