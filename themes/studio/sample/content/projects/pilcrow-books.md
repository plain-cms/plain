Pilcrow Books took over a former ironmonger's with nine thousand second-hand books, a hand-painted sign that still said "Nails & Fixings", and no money to replace the shopfront. The owner, a retired copy-editor, wanted the shop to look like it cared about sentences.

## The challenge

A second-hand bookshop changes its entire stock every few months, so nothing in the identity could depend on what was on the shelves. It had to be applied by the owner herself, with a stencil and a rubber stamp, and it had to sit comfortably next to a hundred-year-old shopfront that the landlord would not let her repaint.

## The approach

We took the pilcrow — the paragraph mark — and the rest of the proofreader's alphabet, and used them as the shop's signage. A caret marks where to insert yourself into the queue; "stet" hangs over the shelf of books that are not for sale. Section signs are set like marginal corrections in red pencil on brown card, and the old ironmonger's sign stays exactly where it was, with a single deletion mark through "Nails".

## The outcome

The shop was picked out by two national papers in its first year, both of which photographed the corrected sign. Stamped paper bags have become the thing customers ask for, and the owner has since marked up the whole high street's Christmas window trail in the same red pencil — at the traders' request.
