Fenn Street Bakery has a queue at seven and is sold out by eleven. For years customers had asked for the recipes, and for years the answer was a photocopied sheet with butter on it. The owners wanted a proper book, and wanted to publish it themselves.

## The challenge

Cookbooks are usually shot in a hired kitchen with a food stylist, over weeks. The bakery could give us its own kitchen between five and six in the morning, before the first bake came out, and a print budget for two thousand copies. The book also had to work propped open on a floury counter, which most cookbooks do not.

## The approach

We photographed nothing that was not about to be sold. Every picture was taken in that one hour, on the bakery's own steel bench, by the light of the proving cabinet — forty mornings in all. The book is section-sewn so it lies flat, with a wipe-clean cover and the quantities set large enough to read from arm's length. Each recipe keeps the bakers' own shorthand in the margin, because that is how they actually teach it.

## The outcome

The first printing sold through the bakery's own counter in nine weeks, with no bookshop distribution at all. A second printing of three thousand followed, and the book now pays for a paid apprenticeship — the first the bakery has been able to offer.
