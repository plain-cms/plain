You do not need to be an athlete, and you do not need to have held a hose. You need to be 18 or over, reasonably fit, able to get to the station within five minutes, and willing to be woken at three in the morning.

## What it asks of you

| Commitment | When | How long |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drill night | Every Monday, 7.30 pm | Two hours |
| Basic training | Eight Saturdays at Easingfold | Six hours each |
| On call | Hours you choose, booked week by week | At least 40 a week |
| Breathing apparatus course | Once, in your first year | Two weeks |

Nobody is paid, but nobody is out of pocket: the fire authority provides your kit and training, and pays travel and lost earnings for courses.

## How to start

1. Come to a Monday drill night and watch. Ask for the station officer.
2. Fill in the short form with the secretary and book a medical with the county.
3. Pass the fitness and ladder tests at Easingfold. We will help you train for them.
4. Start basic training, and carry a pager as a probationer from week four.

## If you employ someone who wants to join

Most of our daytime crew are released by their employers when the pager goes. Calls average twelve a month and most are over within the hour. The station officer will gladly come and explain what is involved.

## Not ready for the engine?

Cadets take 13 to 17 year olds on Wednesday evenings. We also need people who never ride the engine: to run the open day, look after the hydrant records and keep the books.
