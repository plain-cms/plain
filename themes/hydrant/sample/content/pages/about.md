Marrowby Volunteer Fire Brigade was founded in 1891, after the fire at Hebden’s mill, with a hand pump kept in a shed behind the Black Bull. The station on Beck Lane was built by the crew themselves in 1957 and extended in 2004 to fit a modern engine.

## What we do

We are the first crew to any call in Marrowby, Thwaite, Low Ings and the farms up to Hagg Moor. The nearest full-time station is at Easingfold, twenty minutes away, so for most calls we are on our own until they arrive.

Last year’s 143 calls were:

- 41 fires in homes, barns and chimneys
- 27 road collisions
- 22 moorland and grass fires
- 19 calls to help the ambulance service
- 34 others, from flooded cellars to a cow in the beck

## The station

The engine is a 2019 rescue pump carrying 1,800 litres of water, cutting gear and a thermal imaging camera. A four-wheel-drive moorland unit, bought with money the village raised, lives in the second bay from March to October.

Upstairs is a training room that seats thirty. Village groups may use it free on weekday evenings: ask the secretary.

## How we are paid for

The county fire authority provides the engine, the kit and our training. Everything else, from the moorland unit to the kettle, comes from the Marrowby Show, the open day and the collecting tin in the post office. The accounts are read at the annual general meeting and pinned on the station door.
