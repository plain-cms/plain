People are surprised by what a map costs, and then surprised again when I show them where the days go. So here is a recent parish path map, forty kilometres of path on one folded sheet, set out as I would set it out for a client.

| Stage | Days | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Walking | 3 | Every path, both ways, with a field sheet |
| Compiling | 2 | Field notes onto the base survey |
| Drawing | 9 | Paths, contours, names, the cover panel |
| Checking | 2 | A second walk with the draft in hand |
| Corrections | 1 | There are always corrections |

Seventeen days. The drawing is barely half.

## What changes the price

Three things move it more than the size of the area does:

- **How good the existing survey is.** A parish last surveyed in 1974 is a different job from one surveyed last year.
- **How many people must approve it.** One person says yes in a day. A committee says yes in a season.
- **Whether I may walk it in summer.** In February the days are short and the paths are streams.

## What I ask of a client

Tell me who the map is for before you tell me what should be on it. Most of the expensive mistakes I have made began with a list of features and no reader.

— Ines
