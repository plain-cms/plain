Every commission starts the same way: I walk it. All of it, in both directions, before I draw anything. Clients sometimes ask whether that is necessary, now that there are photographs from the air of every field in the country.

It is. A photograph shows a line across a field. It does not say whether the line is a path, a drain or the edge of last year’s crop.

## The field sheet

I carry a print of the best existing map, on waterproof paper, and write on it in pencil. Three kinds of mark:

1. **What is there** and not on the sheet: a new gate, a fallen wall, a bench.
2. **What is on the sheet** and no longer there.
3. **Where I hesitated.** This is the important one. If I stop at a fork and look round, so will everyone else, and that fork needs more drawing than its size deserves.

## Both directions

A walk is two different routes, one each way. The turning that is obvious going out, with the church tower ahead of you, is invisible coming back with the tower behind. I mark every place where I had to look over my shoulder.

Three days of this for a parish. The drawing afterwards takes two weeks, and goes twice as fast as it would have without the walk.

— Ines
