A reader asked what I carry when I walk a survey. Less each year. This is the whole of it.

## In the bag

- **Six pencils**, all 2H. Soft lead smears on waterproof paper, and one pencil is never enough once you have dropped it in a beck.
- **A field sheet**, printed on plastic paper, folded to the size of a paperback.
- **A compass** with a long baseplate. The telephone has one too, and it has been wrong beside every iron gate in Yorkshire.
- **A sixty-centimetre folding rule**, for the width of gates and bridges.
- **Two bulldog clips** and a square of hardboard to clip the sheet to.

## Not in the bag

No tablet. I tried one for a year. It was faster in the dry and useless in the wet, and it made me look at the screen when I should have been looking at the ground.

No camera beyond the telephone. A photograph records what was there. A pencil note records what I thought about it, which is the part I cannot get again.

The bag itself is a postman’s, bought second-hand. It hangs at the hip, where a hand finds a pencil without the eyes having to help.

— Ines
