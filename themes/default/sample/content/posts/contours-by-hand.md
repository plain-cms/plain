Height data comes free from the national survey now, a figure for every two metres of ground, and software will turn it into contour lines before you have lifted your hand from the mouse. The lines are accurate. They are also ugly, and it took me years to say why.

## What the machine draws

A computed contour is faithful to every sample. It wobbles where the data wobbles: round a parked lorry, along a hedge the laser took for a ridge. On a steep bank the lines crowd until they print as a single brown smear.

## What the pen does

So I print the computed lines faintly and draw over them. The pen does three things the software does not:

- It **smooths**, where the wobble is noise and not ground.
- It **exaggerates**, a little, the nick where a stream crosses, so the eye can follow the water uphill.
- It **gives up**: on a crag I stop drawing lines and draw the rock.

None of this is more accurate. All of it is easier to read at arm’s length in poor light, which is where maps are read.

A hill drawn this way takes me about an hour. I have stopped apologising for the hour.

— Ines
