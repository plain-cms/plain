Lumb Mill is a Grade II listed cotton spinning mill of 1843, four storeys of millstone grit on the bank of the Calder. It had stood empty for eleven years when the co-op bought it. 1,450 m², contract value £2.1 million.

## The challenge

The co-op wanted homes its members could afford to rent for good, inside a building the planners rightly wanted kept as it was. The floors are 38 metres long and 12 deep; a flat cut across that depth has a dark middle and a long corridor.

## The approach

We ran the flats front to back instead, each one a full twelve metres deep and two structural bays wide, so every flat has windows on the river and on the yard. The cast-iron columns stand free in the living rooms. The one new stair goes in the old engine house, which kept the mill floors whole.

## The outcome

All fourteen flats were let to co-op members before handover, and the first households moved in that June. Each flat is lined inside the stone with wood fibre and heated from heat pumps in the old boiler house.
