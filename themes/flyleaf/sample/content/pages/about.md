Margaret Tolliver opened the shop in 1984 with the stock of her late uncle's library and a borrowed till. Sam Wren came for a Saturday job in 2009, stayed, and has been a partner since 2017. Joan and Idris work the weekends, and Pilot the cat works the window.

## The new books

We order from the publishers and two wholesalers every Monday and Thursday. Nothing is here because a rep told us it would sell. Each of us keeps a shelf of staff picks by the door, with a card saying why, and those shelves outsell the bestseller table three to one.

## The second-hand books

Most come from houses within twenty miles: a vicar's theology, a sailor's charts, three generations of orange paperbacks. We sort them in the back room on Wednesdays, clean them, price them in pencil, and put the best on this site. What we cannot sell goes to the hospice shop on Quay Road, never to pulp.

## The signed copies

Aldermouth has more writers than fishing boats these days. We hold a reading on the first Thursday of the month, and whoever reads signs what is left of their stock before they go home.

> A bookshop is a room where you find the thing you did not come in for.

That was Margaret's uncle. It is painted over the back door.
