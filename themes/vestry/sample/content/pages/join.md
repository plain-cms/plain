Most people start by coming to a service and staying for coffee afterwards. When you want to do more, there is plenty of room.

## Ways to take part

| What | Who may join | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The choir | Anyone who enjoys singing; juniors from age 8 | Free; music is provided |
| The bell-ringers | Anyone from age 11 who can climb the tower stairs | £10 a year to the county association |
| The flower guild | Anyone, a week or two a year on a rota | Flowers are paid for by the church |
| The electoral roll | Anyone aged 16 or over who lives in the parish or worships here | Free |

## The electoral roll

Being on the roll lets you vote at the annual parochial church meeting and stand for the church council. Ask for a form at the back of church or from the parish office. The roll is revised each year in March.

## Learning to ring

New ringers learn one at a time with a teacher at their side, first handling the rope and then ringing rounds with the band. Most people ring with the band within two or three months of Thursday practices.

## Singing in the choir

Come to a Friday practice and sit with the section that suits your voice. You do not need to read music, though it helps. Junior choristers practise from 6 pm to 7 pm, and their parents are welcome to stay.
