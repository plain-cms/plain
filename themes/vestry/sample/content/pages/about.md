St Botolph’s was consecrated in 1186. The round flint tower is older still: the lowest stage is Saxon, and you can see the herringbone stonework from the churchyard path. The nave was rebuilt in the fifteenth century with money from the wool trade, which paid for the angel roof and the carved bench ends of animals and harvest scenes.

## The bells

The tower holds eight bells, the oldest cast in 1612. All eight were taken down, retuned and rehung in 2026, and they ring for the Sunday morning service, for weddings and on New Year’s Eve. The band practises on Thursday evenings and teaches newcomers from the age of eleven.

## The choir

Twelve adults and nine juniors sing at the Parish Eucharist and at Evensong twice a month. Juniors are paid a small sum for each service and work towards their singing awards.

## The hall

The church hall stands across the lane from the lych gate. It seats eighty, has a kitchen and step-free access, and hosts the playgroup, the Women’s Institute and the Tuesday lunch club. It can be hired for parties and meetings: see [hiring the hall](/hall/).

## Who looks after St Botolph’s

The parish belongs to the Diocese of Norwich and is run by the parochial church council, elected each spring at the annual meeting. The church is kept going by donations, the Christmas fair and the people listed here, who would be glad to hear from you.
