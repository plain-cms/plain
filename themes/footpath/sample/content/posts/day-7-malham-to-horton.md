Malham Cove at eight in the morning, before anyone else: a curved limestone cliff as high as a cathedral, and on top a pavement of grey blocks with ferns growing in the cracks. The Way goes straight up the steps at its side.

Past Malham Tarn, then the long slog up Fountains Fell, which has old coal pits on the top and nothing else to recommend it.

## Pen-y-ghent

From Fountains Fell you see Pen-y-ghent the whole way across, a hill shaped like a crouching lion. By the time I reached its foot the sky behind it had gone the colour of slate. The last scramble up the nose was in rain so heavy I could hear it on my hood above the wind.

On the top, nothing to see but the trig point and a man in shorts who said it was "fresh". Then the long path down to Horton-in-Ribblesdale, where the rain stopped, as it always does, the moment I arrived.
