The club was started in 1923 by eleven quarrymen and a schoolteacher who met in the Old Reading Room on Fell Lane, read out the week's route from a borrowed map, and walked it on Sunday. We still meet in that room, and the first minute book is in the cupboard by the door.

## What we do

- Training walks on Wednesday evenings, about 8 km at a steady pace
- Club walks every Sunday, graded from easy valley rounds to full days on the tops
- A hut weekend at Blea Ghyll about once a month
- Winter skills, navigation and first aid days for members
- One Alpine meet each summer, usually hut to hut

## Blea Ghyll Hut

A former shepherd's bothy at the head of the valley, bought by the club in 1961. It has twelve bunks in two rooms, a wood stove with hot water, a kitchen with gas rings and a drying room. There is no road: it is a 40-minute walk from the Wythe Pass car park.

Members may book any night with the hut warden and bring guests. Other clubs may rent the whole hut midweek.

## Looking after the fells

We give two days a year to footpath repair with the national park rangers, and members carry out what others leave behind.
