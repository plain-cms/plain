I trained as a sign-writer, spent eleven years lettering shopfronts and narrowboats, and started cutting lino in 2016 because I wanted to make the same letter more than once. Since 2019 the press has been my whole job.

## How I work

Every print on this site is cut, inked and pulled by hand, one sheet at a time, on an Albion press built in 1891. Most editions are linocuts; the woodcuts are cut in cherry or lime. Reduction prints are cut from a single block between colours, so when an edition is finished the block is too — there is never a second printing.

Each sheet is numbered, titled and signed in pencil under the print. Proofs that are not part of the edition are marked as proofs, and never sold as edition prints.

## What I make

- Limited editions of linocuts and woodcuts, 20 to 40 prints each
- Labels, packaging and lettering for small makers
- Book covers, title pages and chapter heads
- Posters for events, markets and festivals
- Printmaking workshops for schools and groups

## Commissions

A commission starts with a conversation and a pencil drawing at the size of the block. Lettering and label work usually takes four to six weeks from the first drawing to the first printed sheet; an illustrated book cover, eight to ten.
