The diary was kept by a hill farmer above Widdop from 1921 to 1929, a few lines a day about sheep, weather and prices at Hebden market. Moorside Books wanted a reissue that looked as if it had been printed in the valley, not designed in a city.

## The challenge

The cover had to work in two colours on a cloth binding, and the chapter heads had to reproduce cleanly at 6 cm wide on an uncoated paper.

## The approach

Fourteen blocks in all: a ewe and lamb on the cover, a title page in cut capitals, and a small month block for each chapter — lambing, clipping, the hay, the tup sales. Every block was printed on the Albion, scanned at full size, and sent to the printer as the pulled impression, ink texture and all.

## The outcome

The first printing of 1,500 copies sold out in five months. A signed edition of the cover block, 25 prints at £95, sold out in a week.
