Garlic needs a cold spell to split into cloves, so it goes in now and sits in the ground all winter. I broke two bulbs of ‘Solent Wight’ into cloves and pushed each one in, pointed end up, until the tip was just under the surface: fifteen centimetres apart, in the bed where the potatoes were.

Every other bed that is empty now got the same treatment as last November: a layer of manure from the stable heap and a cover of cardboard. The clay is noticeably better than it was. A fork goes in where a year ago it bounced.

Things I would do again: cardboard instead of digging; radishes with the parsnips; hazel from the reserve. Things I would not: four courgette plants; sowing without a string line; trusting a clear April sky.

That is most of a year on five rods. The robin is still here.
