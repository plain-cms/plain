The cardboard I laid in November had done what Des said it would. Lifting the edge, the clay underneath was dark and crumbly for the top few centimetres, and full of worms that had been eating their way up into the manure all winter. I did not dig; I raked the remains of the cardboard aside and drew two drills with the edge of the hoe.

‘The Sutton’ is a dwarf broad bean, about knee high, which matters on a site where the wind comes straight down the canal. The beans went in five centimetres deep and twenty apart, in a double row with a path either side. Thirty-two beans, and four spares pushed in at the end of the row to fill any gaps.

## The bit I nearly forgot

A string line. Without one my first row wandered like a sheep track, and I pulled it out and started again. Des watched this and said nothing, which is worse than him saying something.

Mice like broad beans as much as I do. I put a length of chicken wire over the row, pegged down at the edges, until the shoots are up.

— Hilary
