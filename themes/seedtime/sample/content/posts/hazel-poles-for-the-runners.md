In February the volunteers at the reserve up the canal cut their hazel coppice and give away what they cannot use. I carried ten rods home on the bus, two metres and a bit, and they have leaned against the shed since, drying.

Runner beans hate cold soil, so I started ‘Scarlet Emperor’ in pots on the kitchen windowsill at the end of April, one bean to a pot. By today they had two proper leaves each and were trying to climb the curtain.

The wigwam is five rods each side of a circle, pushed in deep, tied at the top with garden twine wrapped a dozen times round. One plant to each rod, two to the strongest. They twine anticlockwise, seen from above; tie one the wrong way round and it simply unwinds itself overnight and starts again.

The broad beans are in flower, black and white, and smell faintly of vanilla when the sun is on them.
