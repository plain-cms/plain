The site secretary walked me down to 23B with the key to the gate and a clipboard, and said, kindly, that it had been “resting” for two seasons. It had been resting the way a field rests: under couch grass to the waist, three self-sown raspberry canes and a pallet compost bin that collapsed when I leaned on it.

What it does have is a water butt that holds water, a south-facing slope you could not see until the grass came down, and a neighbour, Des on 24, who has grown leeks on this clay for thirty-one years and offered his opinion before I had my coat off.

## The first job

Not digging. Des was firm about that: dig clay in November and you turn it into roof tiles. Instead I cut everything down with shears, raked it into a heap and covered the top third of the plot with black sheet weighed down with old bricks from the skip. The couch will sulk under it until spring. The rest gets cardboard and a barrow-load of manure from the stable heap by the gate, free to plot holders.

## What I measured

| Bed | Size | State on the day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top, by the path | 6 m × 4 m | Couch, now under sheet |
| Middle | 6 m × 5 m | Grass and raspberry canes |
| Bottom, by the tap | 6 m × 3 m | Bare and wet; standing water after rain |

Three and a half hours, two blisters, one robin that followed the shears the whole way down.

— Hilary
