The black sheet came off the top bed this morning. Under it the couch grass had gone yellow and limp, and most of it came out with a fork in long white ropes. Not all of it — couch never goes all at once — but enough to sow into.

Parsnips want fresh seed, every year. Last year’s packet will give you a few seedlings at best. They also take three or four weeks to show, which is long enough to forget where you sowed them and hoe the row off. The old trick is to sow radishes in the same drill: they are up in a week, mark the line, and are pulled long before the parsnips need the room.

I sowed ‘Gladiator’ in clusters of three, fifteen centimetres apart, with ‘French Breakfast’ radishes in between, and watered the drill before sowing rather than after, so the seed does not wash into a heap at the low end.

Then the waiting starts. Parsnips are the vegetable that teaches you that a plot is not a shop.
