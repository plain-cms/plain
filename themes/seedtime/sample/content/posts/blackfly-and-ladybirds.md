Every tip of every broad bean plant was black this morning, and not with frost. Blackfly cluster on the soft growing points where the sap is richest, so thickly that the stem looks dipped in soot.

There are sprays. I have decided not to use any of them on this plot, which leaves two things. The first is pinching out: the top three or four centimetres of each plant, where the blackfly sit, come off between finger and thumb. The plant has set its lower pods by now and loses nothing. The tops go in a bag and in the bin, not the compost.

The second is waiting. Within a week of the blackfly come the ladybirds, and after them their larvae: small grey crocodiles with orange spots, which eat aphids all day. I counted eleven on the bean row this afternoon.

The first pods are swelling. Some of them are already the length of my finger.
