The seed potatoes came in two paper sacks from the trading hut: a kilo of ‘Charlotte’ for salads and a kilo of ‘Pink Fir Apple’, which Des says is a waste of good ground and which I want anyway, because they look like knobbly fingers and taste of chestnuts.

Chitting means letting them sprout before planting. Each tuber has one end with more eyes — the rose end — and that end goes up. Egg boxes hold them perfectly, six to a box, and the spare-room windowsill is cool and light, which is all they ask.

## What to look for

Short, fat, dark shoots are what you want: purple on the ‘Pink Fir’, green-tipped on the ‘Charlotte’. Long pale ones mean too warm and too dark, and they snap off at planting. I turned the boxes round every few days so they did not all lean towards the glass.

By the end of the month most had two or three sturdy shoots, about a centimetre long. Planting waits until the ground has dried out, which on this clay could be a while.
