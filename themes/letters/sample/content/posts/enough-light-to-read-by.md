The power went out on a Thursday at ten past six, and for the first eleven minutes I did what everyone on the street did: stood at the window to check that it was not only me. It wasn’t. The whole hill was dark down to the lake, and the only lights moving were the ones in people’s hands.

I found the candle we keep for exactly this, and a saucer, and then I stood in the kitchen with nothing to do. The laptop had forty minutes in it. The phone had less, and I wanted it for the morning. So I took the candle to the armchair and picked up the book that had been lying face down on the arm since August, the way you would pick up a conversation with someone you had been avoiding.

A candle lights about one page. I had forgotten this. It is not a romantic fact; it is an optical one. You hold the book inside the small circle or you do not read, and everything outside the circle — the dishes, the unanswered mail, the other four books I was also supposedly reading — goes away, not because you have disciplined yourself but because it is literally not there. The room had been edited down to a page and a hand.

I read for two hours and ten minutes. I know because the candle was a new one and I could see how much of it was gone. I read ninety pages, which is more than I had managed in that book in two months, and I remember them: the brother’s letter, the ferry, the terrible dinner. Nothing interrupted me, including me.

At twenty to nine the refrigerator shuddered and every lamp I own came on at once. It was like being caught. The room came back with all its claims — a dozen surfaces, each with a job on it — and I sat blinking in the middle of them, holding a book I no longer had any excuse to be holding.

I have been trying since to say what the candle did, without getting mystical about it. I think it is only this: attention does not want more willpower, it wants less room. We keep trying to concentrate in rooms lit so evenly that everything in them is equally possible. The old light was stingy. It made you choose a page before it would show you one.

I can’t recommend a power cut. But I have moved one lamp. It sits low, by the armchair, with a shade that throws a circle about the size of an open book, and after supper it is the only one I switch on. The dishes are still there in the dark. They keep.
