I’m Nell Whitcombe. By day I copy-edit other people’s books from a back room in Duluth, Minnesota, with a view of the lake when the window is clean; the letters are where I find out what my own sentences think they’re doing.

Each one is written under the same small rules: it must be about one thing, it must be finished before it’s sent, and it must earn its length — most run about five hundred words because that’s a cup of coffee, not because anyone’s counting. There is no schedule beyond *when it’s done*, which in practice means every four or five weeks and keeps both of us honest.

The name is literal. These are written the way letters used to be — to one reader, with the day’s actual weather in them — and then sent to everyone who asked. If you’d like them in your inbox, the [subscribe page](/subscribe/) says how. If you’d rather just visit, the [archive](/blog/) keeps everything, newest first.

To reply — and you can, every letter takes replies — answer the email, or write to [nell@example.com](mailto:nell@example.com). I read all of it, and the best letters usually started as someone’s answer.

— Nell
