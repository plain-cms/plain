The society began in the winter of 1971, when an evening class on local history at the village school refused to stop meeting. Its first project was to copy every gravestone in St Bridget’s churchyard onto record cards. Those 1,140 cards are still the first drawer of the archive.

## The Old Forge Museum

The forge on Mill Street stopped working in 1962. The society leased it in 1993 and opened it as a museum the next spring, with the hearth, anvil and bellows where the last smith left them. Displays change each year. Entry is free and the museum is run entirely by volunteer stewards.

## The archive

The archive room behind the forge holds:

- 9,000 typed record cards, indexed by name, place and subject
- the parish magazine from 1890 to 1939
- 212 glass lantern slides and some 4,000 photographs
- school log books, sale particulars and farm accounts lent by families
- 61 recorded interviews from the oral history project

The card index is still the way in. Ask us to search it by e-mail, or come on a Wednesday morning by appointment.

## Haverleigh Notes

Our journal comes out in spring and autumn, with the programme, new finds and one long article. Members receive it free; back issues are £2 at the museum.
