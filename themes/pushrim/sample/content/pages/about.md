Haverleigh Ability Sport began on a Thursday evening in October 2012, when
Joanne Pike, then head of PE at Haverleigh High School, borrowed six sports
chairs and asked Marcus Adeyemi, who had played wheelchair basketball for
fifteen years, to show her pupils how. Eleven people came. The next week,
twenty.

Today we run sessions in four school sports halls on four days a week:
wheelchair basketball, boccia and goalball, and a schools boccia league.
3,186 players have been on court with us since that first evening. Some
come for an hour on Tuesdays; some have gone on to play for their region.

## The people

- **Joanne Pike**, director and head coach
- **Priya Raval**, sessions coordinator
- **Marcus Adeyemi**, wheelchair basketball coach
- **Gordon Hale**, chair of trustees

Forty-eight volunteers coach, referee, drive the minibus, keep the chairs
rolling and run the tea urn.

## Accountability

Our accounts are checked by an independent examiner each year and sent to
anyone who asks. Of every pound we were given last year:

| Share | Where it went |
| --- | --- |
| 78p | Sessions: hall hire, coaching, chairs and equipment |
| 9p | The minibus and drivers' expenses |
| 8p | Fundraising |
| 5p | Insurance, accounts and running the office |

Haverleigh Ability Sport is a registered charity in England and Wales,
governed by five trustees who are all players, parents or coaches.
