Jean Ibbotson opened the shop in 1987 as a haberdashery: zips, ribbon, elastic by the yard and four hundred drawers of buttons. Her daughter Ruth came back to Kirkby Ainsley in 2014 with a dye pot and a degree in textile chemistry, and the back room has smelled faintly of vinegar ever since.

Jean still does Saturdays. Ask her for a button to match and she will have the drawer open before you finish the sentence.

## Where the wool comes from

The fleece is Bluefaced Leicester and Swaledale from four farms within twenty miles of the shop. It is scoured and spun for us at Harlow Beck Mill, forty minutes away, and comes back as undyed hanks of 100 grams.

## How it is dyed

Ruth dyes ten hanks to a pot. The blues come from a woad vat that she keeps going from September to May; each dip in the vat darkens the wool a little, so a pale shade has one dip and the deepest has five. Everything is rinsed until the water runs clear and dried on racks over the stove.

> A dye lot is a promise that these ten hanks went through the same pot on the same day. It is not a promise that the next ten will look the same.

## The name

Tansy gives yellow and woad gives blue. Between them, and a little madder, a dyer can make most of the colours of the dale.
