We're a small, independent hearing practice at 3 Calder Row: two audiologists and a front desk that answers the phone. Appointments are long enough for you to ask everything, nobody talks over you, and we write down what we found so you can read it again at home.

## What most people come in for

- [Hearing tests](/care/hearing-tests/) — forty-five minutes, with the results explained on the chart in front of you
- [Hearing aid fitting](/care/hearing-aid-fitting/) — sixty days to try them at home before you decide
- [Hearing aid repairs](/care/hearing-aid-repairs/) — new tubes and domes while you wait at the counter
- [Ear wax removal](/care/ear-wax-removal/) — gentle microsuction, no water and no syringing
- [Home visits](/care/home-visits/) — on Friday afternoons, for anyone who can't easily get to us

[Everything we do, and what each appointment costs](/care/)

## Your first visit

You'll sit down with an audiologist, not a sales desk. We ask how your hearing is getting on in the places that matter to you — the kitchen table, the bus, the phone — then look in your ears and test your hearing in our quiet room. Before you leave, we show you your audiogram and tell you in plain words what it means.

Most people find they don't need hearing aids yet. If you do, there is no pressure to buy anything on the day.

## Bring someone along

Hearing loss is something the whole household lives with. You are welcome to bring a husband, wife, son or friend to any appointment: a familiar voice is useful in the test, and two people remember more of what was said.
