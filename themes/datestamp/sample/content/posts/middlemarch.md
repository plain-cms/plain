The library’s copy is the fat paperback with the cracked spine, and it has been out to somebody in this town at least forty times. I counted the stamps on the slip before I started. I added mine.

I first read *Middlemarch* at nineteen and thought it was about Dorothea’s bad marriage. At sixty-four I think it is about everybody else’s: Lydgate’s, the Bulstrodes’, Fred and Mary’s long engagement, which is the only happy thing in the book and is happy because both of them are patient.

## What it does

Eliot stops the story to tell you what a character is thinking, then tells you what the character next to them is thinking about the first one, and somehow it never feels like a lecture. It feels like sitting in a crowded kitchen and being able to hear everyone at once.

> If we had a keen vision and feeling of all ordinary human life, it would be like hearing the grass grow.

The chapters about the railway surveyors drag. Everything else earns its weight.

**Verdict:** renew it twice and pay the fine. Only the second book ever to earn it.
