I read this on the bus to Carmarthen and back, which was a mistake. The chapter where Uncle Podger hangs a picture had me laughing so hard that the driver asked if I was all right, and I went two stops past the market.

Three young men take a skiff up the river from Kingston to Oxford and back. That is the whole of it. Along the way they fail to open a tin of pineapple, quarrel about who does the work, and let the dog, Montmorency, fight a tea-kettle.

Between the jokes, Jerome stops to write a page of earnest scenery or a little history of Magna Carta, and these bits are dreadful. Skip them; he would not mind. He seems to have put them in because he thought a book ought to have some.

It was published in 1889 and not a single joke has gone off.

**Verdict:** return it early, and lend your own copy to someone who needs cheering up.
