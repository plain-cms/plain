A yellow diamond goes missing from a girl’s bedroom on the night of her eighteenth birthday, and the story is told by one witness after another, each with their own reasons for getting it wrong.

The first narrator, Gabriel Betteredge, the house steward, is the reason to read it. He opens *Robinson Crusoe* at random whenever he needs advice, and he is never once disappointed. His section is the best hundred pages of the book and I was sorry when he handed over.

Some of what follows creaks. Miss Clack, the religious cousin, is a joke that goes on for too long, and the solution depends on a medical theory that would not survive a modern doctor. But the detective, Sergeant Cuff, who would rather talk about roses than crime, is a delight, and every later detective owes him something.

**Verdict:** renew it. You will want the extra week.
