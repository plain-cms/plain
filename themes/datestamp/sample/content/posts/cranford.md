Cranford is a small town in Cheshire where the ladies pretend that nobody has any money, and everybody knows exactly how little everybody else has. They take tea, they play cards for small stakes, and when one of them loses her savings in a bank failure the others club together in secret to keep her afloat.

It started life as magazine pieces and it shows: the chapters wander, and the story only really gathers itself in the second half. I didn’t mind. Miss Matty buying her tea from a shop she will not admit to keeping is worth any amount of wandering.

Gaskell is better known for *North and South*, which I reviewed in June. This is the gentler book, and in some ways the braver one: it is about people being good to each other without ever saying so.

**Verdict:** renew it, and read the last three chapters twice.
