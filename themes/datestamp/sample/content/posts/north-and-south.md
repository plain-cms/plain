Margaret Hale’s father leaves the Church over a matter of conscience and moves the family to Milton, a cotton town that is Manchester in all but name. Margaret hates it, and she especially hates John Thornton, who owns a mill and thinks the workers should be grateful for their wages.

It is a love story, and a good one, but it is also the most serious book I know about a strike. Gaskell lets both sides speak. The union men are not saints and the masters are not villains, and the people who suffer most are the ones with no say at all.

The ending is rushed; Gaskell was writing it in weekly parts and Dickens, her editor, was hurrying her. You can feel the pages running out. I forgave her.

**Verdict:** renew it, and don’t watch the television version first.
