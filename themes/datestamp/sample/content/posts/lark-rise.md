Lark Rise is a hamlet on the Oxfordshire border where the men work the fields for ten shillings a week and the women keep a pig, a garden and twelve children on it. Flora Thompson grew up there, and wrote it down fifty years later when the life she remembered had gone.

There is hardly a story. There is the pig-killing, the harvest home, the May garland, the day the first bicycle came through. There is the travelling packman and what he sold, and what everything cost, to the farthing.

What makes it more than a list is that Thompson never pretends the poverty was charming. The children are hungry in winter. The old people dread the workhouse. And still the place is full of songs, and gossip, and people being decent to one another because they cannot afford not to be.

My mother read it to me when I was nine. I read it again this summer with her copy on the shelf and the library’s in my hands.

**Verdict:** renew it twice and pay the fine. The first book to earn it.
