Charles Pooter lives at The Laurels, Brickfield Terrace, works as a clerk in the City, and keeps a diary because, as he says, he fails to see why he should not, simply because he does not happen to be a somebody.

Everything goes wrong for him on a very small scale. The tradesmen are rude. His son changes his name and gets engaged to unsuitable women. He paints the bath red and it comes off on him. He makes a pun at dinner and nobody laughs, so he writes it down so that we can.

It is very funny, and then, about two-thirds through, it is suddenly rather moving. Pooter is a snob and a bore, and he loves his wife without any fuss at all.

It is a short book. I read it in two evenings and had nearly three weeks left over, which I spent on the Grossmiths’ drawings.

**Verdict:** return it early, and smile at the next man you see painting a bath.
