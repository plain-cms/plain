Constance stays in the shop in the Potteries. Sophia runs off with a commercial traveller and ends up keeping a boarding house in Paris through the siege. The book follows each of them from girlhood to old age, and nothing much happens to either of them except their lives.

Bennett writes about money better than anyone I know. You always know what the shop takes on a Saturday, what the lodgers pay, what a new mantle costs. It sounds dull. It is the reason you believe every page.

The Paris section is too long by about sixty pages, and the siege is told from so far off that it barely registers. But the last chapters, when the sisters are old and back under one roof, made me put the book face down on the kitchen table for a while.

**Verdict:** return it on time, and then borrow it again in ten years.
