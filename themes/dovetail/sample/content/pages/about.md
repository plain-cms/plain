Alan Pennock served his time with a firm of shopfitters in the 1970s and set up on his own in 1989, in what had been the wheelwright’s shop on Tanner Row. Ruth grew up sweeping its floor. She trained in furniture making, came back in 2011, and now does most of the drawing and all of the cabinet work. Dev Kapoor joined as an apprentice in 2021 and hangs a door faster than either of us.

## What we promise

- **One written price.** Making, fitting and finishing in a single figure, agreed before any timber is ordered.
- **A drawing before a saw cut.** You see every piece to scale and can change your mind while it is still pencil.
- **Made here.** Nothing is bought in flat-packed and passed off. If it has our name on it, we cut it.
- **A tidy house.** Dust sheets down, the chop saw outside, and the vacuum round before we leave each day.
- **We come back.** Wood moves in its first winter. One return visit to ease doors and drawers is included.

## The workshop

A panel saw, a planer-thicknesser, a spindle moulder and three benches. Dovetails on drawers are cut by hand, because on a short run it is quicker than setting up a jig, and because people open a drawer and look.

We buy oak, ash and tulipwood from Harland’s yard in Ashby Moor and choose the boards ourselves. Sheet material is birch plywood. We do not use chipboard carcasses.

## Qualifications and insurance

- **Trade qualified** in bench joinery (Alan, Dev) and furniture making (Ruth).
- **Members of the county joiners’ guild** since 1996.
- **£5 million public liability insurance.** Ask and we will send the certificate.
- **Building regulations.** Staircases are drawn to Part K, and fire doors are hung to the maker’s certificate.
