Frank Pennock opened the shop on Weavers Row in 1961, when prescriptions were still weighed out on brass scales. His granddaughter Ruth runs it now. The scales are on a shelf behind the counter; very little else from 1961 is, except the habit of knowing people by name.

We are an independent pharmacy. That means we choose what we stock, we can take the time to talk, and when you ask for the pharmacist you get one — there is one on duty every minute we are open.

## How we work

- **Ask first, buy second.** We would rather tell you that you don’t need anything than sell you a box.
- **Out of stock is our problem.** If a medicine is short, we ring round and phone you when it’s in, rather than send you away with the slip.
- **A quiet word is always possible.** The consultation room has a door that shuts. Ask for it whenever you’d rather not talk across the counter.

## Who you’ll meet

- **Ruth Pennock**, pharmacist and owner. Twenty years a pharmacist, and the person to ask when a medicine and a diagnosis don’t seem to add up.
- **Daniel Osei**, pharmacist. Runs the vaccinations and the travel clinic, and keeps a map of the world on the consultation-room wall for working out which jabs you need.
- **Kerry Whitlam**, pharmacy technician. Makes up the weekly medicine trays and will ring you if your repeat prescription hasn’t come through from the surgery.
- **Joan Haigh**, counter assistant. Thirty-one years at the counter. If she doesn’t know the answer she knows who does.
- **Arif Mahmood**, delivery driver. Out on Tuesday and Friday afternoons with prescriptions for people who can’t get in.

## Keeping it honest

If what you describe at the counter needs a doctor, we will say so plainly and tell you which service to call. If it can safely wait, we will tell you that too.
