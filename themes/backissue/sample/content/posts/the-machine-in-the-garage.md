The duplicator lives in my garage, under a dust sheet, between the bikes I am supposed to be mending and a freezer that came with the house. It was made in 1974 and it weighs about as much as a small motorbike. I bought it from a church in Stevenage that was clearing out its back room, for £40 and a promise to collect it the same day.

## How it works

A duplicator is a kind of very patient printing press. You cut a stencil: a thin waxed sheet with tiny holes wherever the page should be dark. Ours are cut on an electronic stencil cutter that came from the same church, in the same van. The stencil wraps round a drum filled with ink. The drum turns, paper feeds under it, and ink is pushed through the holes onto the paper, one sheet at a time.

One drum holds one colour. So every page of the zine goes through twice:

1. The blue pass: all the words, every page, the whole run.
2. Wash the drum, which takes an hour and most of a bottle of washing-up liquid.
3. The pink pass: the blocks of colour and the shadows on the titles.

## What goes wrong

The second pass never lands quite where the first one did. The paper shifts a millimetre in the feed, or the drum warms up, or the weather changes and the paper takes on damp. So the pink sits a little off the blue on every sheet, by a different amount on every sheet, and where the two inks cross you get a third colour that neither of us chose.

We used to fight it. Now it is the house style. Priya says it looks like the towns: two plans laid on top of each other, never quite lining up.

## The run

Issue 4 is two hundred copies, sixteen pages each, which is six thousand four hundred passes through the machine. It took us three weekends. The garage smells of ink until Christmas.
