*Joan Pettit has lived in the same house in Harlow for sixty-six years. She talked to Dom in her front room in February. This is her account, shortened, in her words.*

We had two rooms over a bootmaker’s on the Roman Road. Mum, Dad, me, my brother Alfie, and Nan on the settee. The toilet was in the yard and you shared it with the bootmaker, who was not a careful man.

Dad got the job first. A factory making lenses, out in a place none of us had heard of. If you had the job, you got the house: that was how it worked. He came home with a key on a bit of string and put it on the table and nobody said anything for a long time.

## The van

We went out on a Saturday in a removals van with Dad’s friend driving. Me and Alfie sat in the back with the wardrobe. It took two hours, and when they opened the doors it was fields, and mud, and a row of new houses with no gardens yet, just clay with planks across it.

> Mum walked in and turned on every tap in the house. Hot and cold. Then she sat on the stairs and cried, and we did not know if it was good crying.

It was good crying. She told me years after.

## The first night

What I remember is the quiet. In Bethnal Green there was always a cart, or a train, or the people upstairs. In Harlow you could hear the wind in the scaffolding. Alfie would not sleep, so I let him in with me, and we lay there listening to nothing.

In the morning there was frost on the inside of the windows and a man from the Development Corporation at the door with a list. He asked Mum if everything was satisfactory. She said it was the best house in England. He wrote that down.
