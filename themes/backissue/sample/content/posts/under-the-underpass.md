The planners of Runcorn put the cars on top and the people underneath. Every main road on the new side of town has a footpath that dips under it, through a tunnel just wide enough for a pram and a bike to pass, lit by strip lights behind wire mesh.

I set out at seven on a Saturday in March with a paper map from 1975 and a flask, and walked every underpass I could find until it was dark. I counted thirty-one.

## What is down there

Mostly what you would expect: puddles, a shopping trolley, a lot of names in marker pen. But also murals. In the late seventies the schools were given the tunnels to paint, and some of the paintings are still there under the later ones, if you look: a blue whale near the Busway, a row of dancing tins of beans, a football team in the colours of a club that no longer exists.

The best one is near the old health centre. A whole wall of tiles, each painted by a different child in 1979, each one a house. Most of them have a chimney with smoke coming out, which almost none of the houses above them had.

## Sound

Underpasses have their own acoustics. A bicycle bell in one sounds like a church. Two teenagers I passed near the shopping city were using one as a recording studio, one of them rapping, the other holding up a phone. They stopped when they saw me, then waved me through, then started again.

## Thirty-one

My feet hurt for two days. I have given the map, with all thirty-one marked in red biro, to the library, who were polite about it. If you know one I missed, write in.
