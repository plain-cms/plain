In the 1952 report that planned our part of Harlow, the people who would live in it are called “overspill population”. Not families, not Londoners, not tenants. Overspill: what runs over the side when a pan is too full.

Dom found the word on a stall at the market, in a council pamphlet with a cover the colour of weak tea. We read it in the café by the bus station and laughed, and then we stopped laughing, because his grandparents were in that pan. So were Priya’s, twenty years later, arriving from Kampala by way of a hostel in Tottenham.

## What we want from this

Most of what gets written about the new towns is written from outside: architecture books with good photographs of empty squares, newspaper pieces about decline, the occasional documentary in which somebody from London gets off the train and looks brave. Some of it is fair. Almost none of it is by anybody who grew up here.

So this is a zine by people who did. It will be about the towns as they are lived in: the underpasses and the roundabouts, the neighbourhood pubs and the sculptures on the greens, the families that came and the families that left.

## Why paper

Because the towns were planned on paper, and argued about on paper, and most of that paper is now in boxes at the county record office. Because a zine on a kitchen table gets read by people who would never open a link. And because Dom found the duplicator before he found the pamphlet, and it seemed a waste not to use it.

We will put every piece online too, the week each issue is printed, so nothing goes out of print.

— Priya and Dom
