The Development Corporations that built the new towns kept everything: plans, minutes, invoices, and the letters their tenants wrote to them. Ours are in forty-three grey boxes in the county record office in Chelmsford. You can read them at a long table, in pencil only, with the radiators ticking.

I went every Wednesday in January. These are some of the letters, copied out as they were written, with only the names left out.

> Dear Sirs, the house is very nice but there is no bus to the station before 7.40 and my husband starts at 7. Is it intended that he walk.

> I wish to complain about the noise of the cockerels kept by the people at number 12. We did not move here from Hackney to live on a farm.

> We have been in our new house three weeks and my children have been well for three weeks, which has not happened before. I do not know who to thank, so I am writing to you.

## The replies

The Corporation answered every one. The carbons are in the boxes too, typed on thin paper, signed by a Mr Haydon of the Housing Department, who seems to have spent the 1950s being reasonable. The bus was moved to 6.50. The cockerels were, after a second letter, rehomed.

To the mother of the well children he wrote: *Thank you for your letter, which has been passed round the office.*

## Why read them now

Because the towns are being argued about again, and the arguments are mostly about buildings. The letters are about something else: buses and noise and children and who to thank. That is still what people write about, if anyone asks them.
