A dessert apple with rough gold skin and a flavour people compare to hazelnuts. It flowers early but the blossom stands a frost better than most, which is why it has been grown this far north for a hundred and fifty years.

## Planting

Stake it for the first three years and keep a metre of ground clear of grass around the trunk. On MM106 it suits a lawn or a large border; ask us for M26 if you want a smaller tree for a fence or a tub.

## A partner for it

It sets a fair crop alone and a better one beside 'James Grieve' or 'Discovery'. We keep both.
