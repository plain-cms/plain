Harrowdale Mountain Rescue began in the winter of 1968, after a shepherd
spent a night out on Scar Fell with a broken leg while a dozen men from the
Kettlethwaite quarry searched for him by lamplight. Dr Alan Rigg, the village
GP, put a stretcher in the back of his car, and the team has been on call
ever since.

Today there are 42 hill members and about twenty base and support
volunteers. Hill members carry a pager day and night and train every
Wednesday evening. None of us is paid.

## Accountability

Our accounts are checked by an independent examiner and filed every year.
Of every pound we spent in 2025:

| Share | Where it went |
| --- | --- |
| 81p | Vehicles, radios, medical kit and training |
| 13p | Running the base: heating, light, insurance |
| 6p | Fundraising, mostly collecting tins and printing |

In 2025 we received £71,300 and spent £68,000. The surplus goes towards
replacing the older of our two rescue vehicles, which turns twenty in 2027.

Harrowdale Mountain Rescue Team is a registered charity, number 0000000, and
a member of the regional mountain rescue association.
