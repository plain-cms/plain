Ted Hobday started with a hand barrow of his father's cauliflowers outside the Corn Exchange in 1961 and had a proper pitch on the Market Place by the following spring. His daughter Lorraine has run the stall since 1994, and her son Callum does the buying. Sheila has worked the scales on Saturdays for longer than any of us can prove.

## Where it comes from

We buy direct from growers we have dealt with for years, and we can tell you which field most of it came out of.

| Grower | What they send us |
| --- | --- |
| Gedney Drove Farm | Potatoes, carrots, beetroot, leeks |
| Tydd Bank Fruit | Strawberries, raspberries, Victoria plums |
| Carr Dyke Farm | Hens' eggs and duck eggs |
| Terrington Marsh Growers | Asparagus, greens, sprouts, cauliflowers |
| Leverington Orchards | Bramleys, Cox's, Conference pears |

Bananas, citrus and anything else that will not grow in Lincolnshire come from the wholesale market at Spalding, bought by the box at half past four in the morning.

## How we sell it

By the pound, the bunch, the bowl and the box. The scales read in pounds and kilos and are checked by Trading Standards every year. The price on the card is the price you pay, and it is the same whether you buy one or twenty.

## What we do with what is left

Anything that is ripe today and will not be tomorrow goes in the pound-a-bowl bowls at the end of the stall. What is left after that goes to the food bank at St Botolph's on Saturday evening.
