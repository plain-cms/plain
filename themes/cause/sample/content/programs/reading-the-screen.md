Almost everything that used to be a paper form is now a screen: the benefits
renewal, the pharmacy refill, the school's messages about a child. A learner
who has just won the page can lose it all over again at a login box.

Reading the Screen is six weeks in the central library's computer room, on
the learner's own phone wherever possible, because that is the screen they
will be alone with afterwards. We practice the real things — a password that
can be remembered, a form that times out, an email from a landlord — and
nobody touches the learner's device but the learner.

**Who it serves:** current and former learners, plus anyone referred by the
benefits office or the credit union. Reading level matters less than nerve;
we start with whichever screen is causing the most trouble this week.

**A typical session:** ninety minutes on Thursday mornings or Monday
evenings, six learners and two tutors. The first half is one shared task;
the second half is each person's own errand.

**What it costs us:** $5 per learner-hour, most of it mobile data and loaner
tablets for learners without a phone of their own.
