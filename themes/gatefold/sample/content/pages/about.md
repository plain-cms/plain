Dead Wax opened in 2005 in what had been a saddler's shop, with eleven crates of soul and jazz from Maggie Thackray's own shelves. Maggie still prices every record. Joss Okoro joined in 2014 and looks after reggae, the singles boxes and the listening deck.

The name is the trade's word for the smooth band between the last groove and the label, where the pressing plant scratches its matrix number. It is the first place we look when a record comes in.

## Where the records come from

Most of the second-hand stock is bought over the counter or from house clearances within an hour's drive. We pay cash or shop credit on the day, and we will tell you plainly when a collection is worth more at auction than we can give for it. New records come from four distributors and straight from a dozen small labels.

## What the grades mean

Grades are given after a clean and a full play, vinyl first and sleeve second.

| Grade | Vinyl | Sleeve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NM | Plays as new, no marks you can hear | Sharp corners, no wear |
| VG+ | Light marks, an odd quiet tick | Slight shelf wear |
| VG | Crackle in quiet passages, never a skip | Ring wear or a small split |
| G | Plays through with steady noise | Worn, taped or written on |

Anything below G goes in the pound box by the door, sold as seen.
