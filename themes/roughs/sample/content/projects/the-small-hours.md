Fifty-two drawings, one a week from July to June, each about 60 millimetres wide, for Moira Quist's column on who is awake at four in the morning: bakers, night nurses, owls, a man walking a very old dog.

## The brief

The magazine wanted a small drawing that a reader would recognise across the page before reading a word, every week, for a year — and that would survive being printed on newsprint at the size of a stamp.

## The roughs

The column arrives on Monday morning. By lunchtime I send three thumbnails; the editor picks one, and the finished drawing goes by Wednesday noon. Over the year the drawings lost almost all their detail and gained a lot of sky.

## The finished work

The run ended with the June issue. The editor has bound all fifty-two drawings into a small calendar for the magazine's readers, printed in the same black and blue.
