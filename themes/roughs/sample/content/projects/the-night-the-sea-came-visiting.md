Ada Mensah's text is 480 words long and almost all of it happens in the dark: a girl called Effie, a high tide, and a town that wakes up to find the sea has come up the high street to look around.

## The brief

Larkspur wanted a bedtime book that was exciting without being frightening, for children of four to seven. The flood had to feel like a visitor, not a disaster — and the book had to work in a printing that uses one special blue on top of the four colours.

## The roughs

The first dummy had the water rising steadily from spread to spread, and it read like a warning. In the second, the sea arrives all at once at the page turn on pages 12–13, and from there it is curious rather than dangerous: fish looking in at the bakery, a crab on the bus shelter. Everything else followed from that page turn.

## The finished work

Twenty-seven paintings in pencil and gouache, the special blue kept for the water alone, and endpapers that show the town before and after. The first printing is 12,000 copies, with French and Dutch editions agreed.
