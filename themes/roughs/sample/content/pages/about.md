I grew up in Cromer, studied illustration in Cambridge and spent four years as a junior designer at a children's publisher before going freelance in 2019. I have illustrated nine picture books, written one of them, and drawn for magazines and newspapers most weeks since.

## How I work

Every job starts on a dummy sheet: the whole book, or the whole page, drawn as rows of thumbnails no bigger than a stamp. That is where the pacing is decided — where a page turn lands, which spread goes quiet, where the big picture goes. Only then do I draw the roughs at size, and only when the roughs are agreed do I start the finished art.

I draw in pencil and paint in gouache, then scan and tidy the pages myself, so a publisher receives print-ready files at 600 dpi with the bleed already in place.

## What I do

- Picture books — 24, 32 and 40 pages, as illustrator or as author and illustrator
- Covers and endpapers
- Non-fiction for children, with diagrams and maps
- Editorial illustration — feature openers, columns and spot drawings
- Posters, programmes and hand lettering
- Maps and interpretation boards

## Who for

Larkspur Children's Books, Bramble & Finch, The Eastern Ledger Saturday Magazine, Hearthside magazine, the Wensum Story Festival, the Fenland Reed Trust and the Owl & Lantern Bookshop.

## Timings

A 32-page picture book takes about nine months from the first dummy to finished art. A feature opener takes three to five working days; a weekly spot is drawn on Monday and delivered by Wednesday noon.

## Start a project

Write with the format, the deadline and, for a book, the text if you have it. For publishing I am represented by Lantern Lane Agency, who handle contracts and fees; editorial and local commissions come straight to me.
